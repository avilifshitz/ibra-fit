Three ranks. Five conditions each, and every one is measured. You pick your target rank. The rank you've reached is set at the weekly close, by the numbers.

Why Hebrew

The names come from Hebrew: ben chayil, ish chayil, gibor chayil. Chayil is the Bible's word for strength and valor. IBRA was born in Hebrew, and its ranks carry that meaning.

FFMI: your lean mass in kg, divided by your height in meters squared. Above 25 is very rare for natural lifters, so the ladder tops out at 23.5.

Posture — the fourth condition

Back of the head to the wall. Back against a wall, heels touching it, and check what fits between the back of your head and the wall: a fist · two or three fingers · one finger · touching.

The bar: one finger (about 2 cm) or touching. The same bar for all three ranks.

On top of that, every 3 weeks a side photo measures your head angle, like “36° → 30°” on the home page. The angle shows whether you're improving. The wall sets the rank.

Fitness — the fifth condition

Level 1: one pull-up · 2 dips · 3 push-ups · 4 sit-ups · 5 squats. Level 2: everything doubled. Climb level by level to your peak, then work back down to 1. No way down, no pyramid.

Or: the skills test

The fitness condition is also met without the pyramid, when every skill of the rank holds. The app teaches each skill as a progression, with an illustration and coaching cues for every step.

How it's measured

  • Tape measure — shoulders and waist. Waist every week, shoulders every three weeks.
  • Caliper — three skinfolds, every three weeks. That gives body fat, and body fat plus height gives FFMI.
  • The wall — the head-to-wall test, in the posture routine.
  • The pyramid — in the first and the last week of every plan. In the app, never typed in. The skills — when the coach slots them in.

No tape — no ratio. No caliper — no body fat or mass. No height — no mass. What isn't measured isn't counted, and isn't guessed.

When your rank updates

  • Only at the weekly close. Mid-week you see “3 of 5” — not a rank name.
  • Old measurements don't count. Six weeks for body and posture, eight for fitness.
  • A rank only drops after two closes in a row measured below it. A week without measurements isn't a step back.

The limits of accuracy

A tape moves by a centimeter, a caliper by a percent or two. That's why the slow measures are taken every three weeks, and a single week is read as a trend, not a verdict.

The thresholds aren't medical advice, and they're not a measure of anyone's worth. They're a fitness standard, so you know what it takes and where you're heading. Pick your target by your condition, your age and your health. None of it is required.
