Who's behind IBRA

IBRA is developed and distributed by Avraham Yoel Lifshitz (sole proprietor), 2 HaShahar St., Binyamina, Israel. Contact: support@ibra.fit, or “Write to us” in your profile.

The sentence that organizes everything

IBRA runs no server. No account. No analytics. No ads. No third-party SDKs. Your data exists in two places only: your device, and your iCloud.

What the app stores on your device

Profile (name, nickname, age, sex, height) · goals · gear, injuries, limitations, allergies and food preferences · the plan your AI wrote · your log: sets, meals, habits, routines, flexibility measurements, HIIT scores, the fitness ladder, how you felt and pain · body measurements · body photos and inspiration photos · weekly-close packages and your coach's replies.

Apple Health (HealthKit)

With your permission, IBRA reads from Apple Health: heart rate, resting heart rate, HRV, VO₂max, sleep, weight, height, body fat, workouts, date of birth and sex, so the briefing and the weekly summary you share can include them. Writing to Apple Health — a workout session, plus weight, body fat, waist and height you measured in the app — happens only if you turn it on in your profile. Health data is never used for advertising, marketing or sale, and is never given to any third party. Sleep and heart rate read from Apple Health, and the weight, height, date of birth and sex read from it, are kept in the app so you can see them, stay on your device and don't go to iCloud; when needed they're read again from Apple Health, where they're backed up in your Apple account. They're never sent to any IBRA server. The average heart rate saved with a cardio workout is part of your workout log, and is backed up with it.

Why, and whether it's required

The only purpose: to build, run and track your training. You're never legally required to provide any data; data you don't provide simply isn't part of the plan or the tracking. No one at IBRA receives your information — only what you choose to send your AI.

Calendar and friends' names

With your permission, IBRA reads only holidays from your calendar, to know about days without a workout. A friend's name you typed when you got a challenge from him stays on your device and appears on the reply card you send him yourself.

Camera

Your body photos stay with you, and are backed up to your private iCloud. The photo-quality check runs on your device. IBRA never uploads them to any server of its own.

iCloud (backup)

The backup is your private iCloud database, in your Apple account. IBRA can't read it. What's backed up: your profile, plan, log, measurements and body photos. What isn't: sleep, heart rate, weight, height, date of birth and sex read from Apple Health, which stay on the device and are read from there again when needed, and device preferences like workout sounds. The briefing you edited also stays on the device only, because it quotes them. The average heart rate saved with a cardio workout is part of your workout log, and is backed up with it. Deleting in the app deletes there too. You can turn it off in Settings › iCloud.

Your AI

IBRA never sends data to an AI. You copy or share it yourself. What you send (the briefing, a weekly-close package, photos) includes age, sex, height, weight, injuries, Apple Health data and body photos, and is covered by the privacy policy of the provider you picked. IBRA is not a party to the conversation.

Purchases

Subscriptions are purchased through Apple; IBRA neither receives nor stores payment details.

YouTube

Demo videos play in YouTube's player. Google may receive your IP address, cookies, and the exercise name you searched. It's the only third party that receives anything, and Google's privacy policy applies.

Apple Watch, the lock screen, the share extension

Live heart rate and an end command pass between the watch and the iPhone. The lock screen shows the running session, without health values. The share extension passes the text you shared to the app. All local.

Retention, deletion, and your rights

Data is kept until you delete it. “Delete everything and start over” in your profile deletes it all, including in iCloud. We have no copy, so viewing, correcting and deleting all happen inside the app. Under Israel's Privacy Protection Law (Amendment 13), body data is information of special sensitivity: consent to store it is given with a separate tap on the consent screen at first launch, and can be withdrawn by deleting.

Children

IBRA isn't meant for anyone under 13, and anyone under 16 uses it with a parent's permission. If we learn that someone under 13 is using it, the instruction is to delete.

Contact and changes

Questions and requests: support@ibra.fit, or “Write to us” in your profile. Database owner: Avraham Yoel Lifshitz. Written requests — through the channel above. Any change to this policy is shown again on the consent screen.
